## 1. Project identity

The long-term system is **Ocean Life Mother**: a tropical marine-and-island life system serving the Stone Money Island / Survivor Palau game. It may eventually coordinate Fish, Bird, Coral, island vegetation, water, habitat, food web, tide/time and rare events. The immediate restart, however, is deliberately narrower:

> **Fish Mother is the only active production focus.**

Bird remains preserved from R02. Coral is delegated to Coral Mother. Ocean water, island terrain and Game Mother are external dependencies and must not be silently rewritten from this branch.

The intended outcome is not a collection of conventional downloaded models. It is a compact, explainable, reproducible **KAOPU life-function system** capable of generating fish form, material, motion and behavior from shared kernels plus species/variant/state data.

## 4. What “distillation” means in this project

Uploaded models, pictures and websites are accelerated study material. They are not the final runtime and not the canonical fish system.

The production pipeline is:

> reference observation → structural understanding → independent function/kernel expression → evidence comparison → accepted Fish Mother output → Game Mother handoff

Final runtime intent:

- no source GLB mesh dependency;
- no source texture-file dependency;
- no source full rig/skin dependency;
- no source full keyframe-track dependency;
- no hidden “converted source mesh” relabelled as a KAOPU function;
- all retained data must have a declared semantic role.

The final system is based on natural structure and movement. Reference assets are temporary observation aids and must eventually be replaceable by natural evidence and the project’s own implementation.

Distillation does **not** mean automatic simplification. It means finding the shortest expression that still preserves the required truth. If a detail cannot be reconstructed from the shared rule, it is either:

- represented by a justified necessary residual;
- left unknown;
- or the current kernel is rejected as insufficient.

## 5. Fish form: required functional interpretation

The user’s core concept is that fish form can be expressed as intersecting, continuous relationships rather than as a stored polygon asset.

A useful body coordinate system must at minimum express:

- longitudinal position from head to tail;
- transverse/vertical cross-section relationships;
- centreline and local orientation;
- width and height functions for each body station;
- local asymmetry and necessary species-specific corrections;
- stable attachment coordinates for mouth, eyes, gill covers and fins;
- time or life-stage parameter where real evidence supports change.

A conceptual body field may be written as a centreline plus width/height fields and local corrections. The exact production equation is not predetermined by this document. What matters is that every visible point has a stable relationship to the body coordinate system, and that anatomy does not become a painted mark.

### Mandatory anatomical structure

Each fish must separately account for:

- head and cranial volume;
- upper and lower jaw, mouth cavity and opening relationship;
- eye globe, cornea/pupil and permitted orientation;
- gill cover position and respiratory movement boundary;
- body trunk and tail peduncle;
- caudal fin attachment and fork/rounding structure;
- dorsal, anal, pectoral and pelvic fin attachment, root, surface and permitted motion;
- skin/scales or non-scaled surface type;
- left/right correspondence and any real asymmetry.

A mouth is not a dark line. A gill is not a painted crease. A fin is not a detached sheet. Eye, gill and mouth movement must follow structural interfaces.

### No conventional asset LOD hierarchy

The system must not store separate high/mid/low fish models as its canonical representation. Near and far views should evaluate the same fish identity at different observation scales:

- near: head, eye, mouth, gill, fin edge, scale and PBR detail;
- medium: body silhouette, fins, major colour regions and motion;
- far: filtered silhouette/colour/coverage or point-like contribution.

This still requires scale-aware evaluation and filtering. “No traditional LOD assets” does not mean infinite detail has zero computational cost.

## 6. Materials, colour and variants

Colour is not the fish identity by itself. Shape, appearance, life stage and action must be independently represented.

A material function may return, in stable body coordinates:

- base colour/albedo;
- alpha/transmission where justified;
- roughness;
- specular/F0 or compatible PBR term;
- normal/microstructure;
- emissive only where genuinely supported;
- scale pattern, stripe, spot, net, border and fin-ray fields;
- body-region mask and life-stage/individual-state modifiers.

Use standard PBR semantics rather than inventing arbitrary colour numbers. Pantone or named colours can be references, but production values must be defined in an explicit colour space.

### Variant taxonomy

Do not mix these categories:

1. Species difference.
2. Sex difference.
3. Juvenile/adult or other life-stage difference.
4. Regional or individual colour morph.
5. Physiological/state change.
6. Lighting/environment effect.
7. Pure engineering colour experiment.

A body may share a shape family while using different appearance recipes, but similarity in a collage does not prove identical topology, species or growth relationship.

## 7. Growth and time

The system must eventually express fish from small to large, but uniform scaling is not accepted as a complete growth model.

A real growth/life-stage implementation needs evidence for changes in:

- head-to-body ratio;
- eye size relative to head;
- mouth and jaw proportion;
- body depth and width;
- tail peduncle and fin proportions;
- colour regions and pattern density;
- scale visibility/material;
- swimming frequency, amplitude, speed and behavior;
- diet, predators, habitat and schooling role.

If evidence exists only for one adult specimen, the system must not invent a juvenile truth from scale alone.

## 8. Motion and actions

A source animation is an observation aid, not a final movement asset.

The shared motion system should eventually separate:

- centreline curvature/amplitude;
- phase delay along the body;
- tail-beat frequency;
- caudal-fin shape and phase;
- dorsal/anal stabilisation;
- pectoral/pelvic-fin control;
- eye orientation;
- mouth/jaw action;
- gill ventilation;
- turn and acceleration control;
- water-relative speed versus world-relative transport.

The system requires stateful actions, not one endless loop:

- steady cruise;
- slow manoeuvre/hover where species-appropriate;
- turn;
- acceleration;
- escape;
- pursuit;
- approach food;
- bite/feed;
- shelter/hide;
- schooling alignment;
- player avoidance;
- return to home/host area;
- rest or species-specific low-activity state.

Animation playback duration alone is not real speed. Without a known physical scale, camera motion and environmental flow, report relative measures instead of fabricated metres per second.

### Natural motion evidence

Natural motion study should prioritise:

- primary research on live animals;
- NOAA, PICRC, National Geographic Pristine Seas and other credible field/agency sources;
- source-matched species, body length, temperature, current and behaviour state;
- explicit uncertainty and domain limits.

The narrow yellowfin motion lesson already established one important principle: tail-beat timing should be based on water-relative swimming, while current affects world transport. That principle may be reused only where the evidence domain is valid; it cannot be applied to all fish.

## 9. Fish-school / BIOS behavior

The user’s intended fish-school system uses local perception rather than every fish observing every other fish.

A future implementation may use:

- a local neighbour set;
- forward sensing for food, route and threat;
- rear/side sensing for group continuity and threat propagation;
- separation, alignment and cohesion as distinct terms;
- role differences between leading, internal and edge individuals;
- memory/state so behaviour does not flicker each frame.

A baked school animation is not autonomous schooling. It can show authored trajectories, but it does not prove food search, threat response, navigation or player interaction.

## 10. Ecological and habitat system

Fish cannot be uniformly scattered across the map. Each species/role requires a habitat and activity envelope.

Core zones to support over time:

- beach / wave-wash boundary;
- reef flat;
- inner reef / lagoon;
- coral patch and shelter zone;
- channel;
- fore-reef slope;
- drop-off / outer deep water;
- open/pelagic water;
- surface-breathing or surface-feeding layer where applicable.

Palau-specific world logic noted by the user:

- the outer reef protects many inner areas from large regular surf;
- nearshore/lagoon water and outer deep water differ sharply;
- beyond the reef it can become very deep;
- channels, tides, seasons and prey movement influence animal presence;
- tuna can conditionally enter reef/channel systems when prey is present;
- presence is probabilistic, not absolute.

### Food-web direction

Target groups include:

- schooling forage fish / sardine-like bait groups;
- tuna;
- barracuda;
- giant trevally (GT);
- sharks;
- snapper groups, including future red/black snapper references;
- small ornamental and coral-associated fish;
- clownfish/anemonefish with host-anemone relationship, not generic coral binding;
- turtles with species/life-stage-specific feeding and surface breathing;
- manta ray as filter feeder;
- eagle ray as benthic forager;
- other rays;
- seabirds and island-life links later.

“Large fish eats small fish” is only a starting constraint. Diet, prey size, life stage, season, depth, time, current, shelter and energetic state must determine actual interactions.

### Player relationship

The player spends much of the survival game observing and interacting with nature. Desired behavior includes:

- reef fish flee as the player approaches;
- they do not teleport;
- after the player leaves and the area is safe, they return to their host/home zone;
- fish remain in water and never swim under sand;
- a notebook can record observations without magically identifying unknown species;
- fishing and observation later connect to Game Mother.

## 11. Physical and environmental constraints

These are foundational and may not be violated for visual convenience:

- fish remain inside water volume;
- fish do not penetrate sand, reef, rock, coral or shoreline;
- visible seabed and collision/constraint seabed read the same world definition;
- wave trough and tide must be considered in vertical clearance;
- whole body and fins matter, not only the actor centre;
- rejected unsafe movement must not teleport the fish;
- fish orientation follows motion;
- current and water-relative movement remain separate quantities;
- world state is independent of camera distance and rendering detail;
- time, units, coordinate frame and datum are explicit.

Environment unknowns must remain unsupported, not silently become zero current, flat seabed or generic noise.
