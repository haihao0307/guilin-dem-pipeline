# R05 work session status — 2026-10-25

## Research conclusions

1. Lace is not one generic construction. Museum sources distinguish needle lace (buttonhole/loop stitch construction, often firmer/crisper) from bobbin lace (crossed/plaited threads, often softer) and describe motifs, filling, joining bars/ground and edge finish as distinct structures.
2. NVIDIA's 2025 ray-wave fabric model and Cornell's micron-resolution work support multi-scale yarn/fiber appearance, view/light-dependent reflection and transmission, and explicit performance approximations. They are woven/knit models; their optical principles are transferable, but their topology and measured performance are not direct evidence for this lace generator.
3. R04's own report says its fill-curvature improvement was visually limited and added about 24.16% total triangles over R03. Therefore the next experiment should not add geometry density by default.

## Not yet proven

- No real browser/WebGL visual acceptance in this session.
- No same-camera, same-light rendered R04-vs-R05 screenshot comparison available from this run.
- No target GPU frame-time, mobile-device or full garment physics/collision evidence.
- No new fixed HTTPS R05 deployment.

Therefore this is an implemented research candidate, not a visual pass. The new cross-section displacement is deliberately small (the A/B geometry test measures a maximum position delta around 0.001 cm on its test yarn; the cross-section spread test reports 0.0016 cm on a 0.1 cm radius test yarn). If it cannot be seen beneficially under a controlled macro/grazing-light view, it should be rejected or replaced by a better shader-based multi-ply representation rather than amplified blindly.

## Regression correction after correlated shader-phase change

The first CI run after adding phase-correlated irregularity exposed two test/geometry issues: the exact ellipse-radius assertion accidentally used the default plied profile, and the stronger 1.8% lobe amplitude caused the existing low-resolution swept-face/normal agreement check to fall below its 0.90 bound. The ellipse test now explicitly disables the envelope, and the lobe amplitude is restored to 1.2% with bounded deterministic modulation. The phase-correlated shading breakup remains. The next run must pass both checks before any further visual conclusions.
